Wind Power Lab: How Moving Air Carries Energy

PHY 1020. Change the wind, the blades and the location, and watch what happens to the kinetic energy carried by the air.

Before you start: energy, power and their units

This lab is about how the wind carries energy and how fast a turbine converts it, which is power. Keep these units straight; most mistakes in wind-power problems are unit mistakes.

Energy → joules (J)

Energy is measured in joules. The kinetic energy of moving air is

KE = ½ m v²

with m in kg and v in m/s, KE comes out in J. Example: 1 kg of air moving at 10 m/s carries ½ × 1 × 10² = 50 J.

Power → watts (W)

Power is energy per second, how fast energy is delivered:

P = E / t     1 W = 1 J/s

A turbine rated at 2 MW delivers 2,000,000 J of electrical energy every second.

Prefixes: watts, kilowatts, megawatts

Converting

  • W → kW: divide by 1,000
  • W → MW: divide by 1,000,000
  • kW → MW: divide by 1,000
  • MW → W: multiply by 1,000,000

Example: 408,407 W = 408.407 kW = 0.408407 MW ≈ 0.41 MW.

Joules ↔ watts

  • Energy = power × time: E = P × t (J = W × s)
  • A 3 MW turbine running for 10 s delivers 3,000,000 W × 10 s = 30,000,000 J
  • In the lab, the kinetic energy carried through the blades each second (in J) is the power available (in W)

Watch out: W is a rate (J per second), not an amount of energy.
